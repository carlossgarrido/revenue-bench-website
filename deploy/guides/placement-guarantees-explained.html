Sales Recruiting Placement Guarantee Terms, Compared

By Carlos Garrido, Co-Founder, Revenue Bench27 min read

Placement guarantee terms decide whether the promise covers anything when a hire does not work out. Seven terms carry that weight, from the window and its start date through the remedy, the re-run count, the void conditions, who decides, and what support runs inside the window. The comparison table below sets the market figures beside Revenue Bench's published answer on each one, with the check to run against any agreement. This guide also follows Revenue Bench's re-run from the client's decision through the replacement hire's first day, with the owner, cost, and limit at each stage. It also explains how candidate retention rates are defined and what to put in writing before a multi-year agreement.

What is a placement guarantee, and how does it work?

A placement guarantee is a clause in a recruiter's agreement that sets out what the firm will do if a hire leaves or is let go inside a defined period after the start date. It is the recruiter putting something at risk behind the placement. In practice that promise takes one of three forms: the firm runs the search again at no additional fee, it returns some or all of the fee, or it credits the fee against a future search. The guarantee is where a recruiter tells you, in writing, how much of the outcome they are willing to stand behind. The specifics carry the meaning, and the headline number does not. A guarantee also shifts the economics of the fee itself, which is part of how recruiter fee structures allocate search risk.

What is the standard guarantee period?

There is a market default, and it is 90 days. In Top Echelon's 2019 survey of its recruiting-network members, 90 days was the most common guarantee length at 44.9% of firms. Thirty days accounted for 20.3% and 60 days for 20.0%. At the longer end, six months accounted for 2.4% and one year for 2.3%. The remaining responses were 45 days at 1.9%, other windows at 5.7%, and no response at 2.5%.

A longer window reads as more protection. Length and strength of coverage are separate measurements, so read the number of days and the exclusions as two separate questions. The section below works through what each one is worth.

Is a longer guarantee better?

A window and its exclusions multiply. Inside the first 90 days, a guarantee that excludes nothing covers more situations than a longer guarantee that excludes some. From the end of the shorter window to the end of the longer one, the longer guarantee covers whatever its exclusions permit, while the shorter one covers nothing at all. Both statements hold at the same time, which is why the number of days on its own cannot answer the question.

Long windows are also uncommon. In Top Echelon's 2019 survey, 2.4% of firms used a six-month window and 2.3% used a year. Where a long window carries no exclusions, it is a substantial commitment and worth weighing as one. Length is one of the seven terms in the table below, and the other six decide what the window is worth.

There is one condition under which a longer window is the right thing to ask for. If a full selling cycle in your market runs longer than the guarantee window, the window closes before the hire has completed a single cycle, and 90 days will report whether the hire is working rather than whether the hire can sell in that environment. What a 90-day window can read is the leading evidence: booked meetings, conversations that reach a second step, and whether coaching changes behavior. Our guide on a sales hire who is not working out sets out what that evidence looks like at day 90. Raise the cycle-length question with any firm you are considering, this one included, and get the answer in writing. Revenue Bench publishes 90 days from the hire's first day and does not publish a longer window. What runs inside those 90 days is a dedicated onboarding coach on every placement.

Which firms publish their guarantee terms

Offering a placement guarantee is close to universal, while the terms vary. Top Echelon's 2019 survey of members of its recruiting network found that 95.9% of the recruiters surveyed offered a guarantee of some kind and 4.1% offered none. The sample consists of recruiters in one network describing the terms they offered in 2019, so the finding reports what firms said they offered. It does not report what employers received. Top Echelon does not publish how many recruiters responded.

In September 2026 Revenue Bench read the public websites of 28 sales and executive search firms that a United States buyer would reach through ordinary searches for a sales recruiting firm, and could read 26 of them. Among those 26 readable sites, 11 of 26 (42%) stated that a guarantee exists in any wording, 6 of 26 (23%) stated a specific period in days or months, 7 of 26 (27%) described the remedy in a sentence, 4 of 26 (15%) stated what voids the guarantee, and 1 of 26 (4%) stated when the clock starts. Revenue Bench counted a site in the 7 of 26 remedy total only when the site explained replacement, refund, or credit in a sentence. A further 4 firms used the phrase "replacement guarantee" and described the remedy nowhere, which raises the remedy count to 11 of 26 (42%) under the looser definition.

The three guarantee types

Guarantees come in three shapes. They protect you differently, and the difference is worth knowing before you compare two offers side by side. The table gives you the remedy and the contract terms to check for each type.

Type What you get What to check
Free replacement search The firm runs the search again at no additional fee, up to the number of re-runs the agreement states. The cap on the number of replacements and any delivery terms.
Fee refund (full or prorated) Some or all of the fee comes back if the hire leaves inside the window. Whether it is full or prorated, when it is paid, and how you will fill the open role.
Fee credit The fee is credited toward a future search rather than returned as money. Whether the credit expires, and whether it obligates you to run another search to use it.

What voids a typical guarantee?

A guarantee is only as strong as the list of things that cancel it, and that list sits in the body of the contract rather than on the marketing page. Before you sign anything, ask for the void conditions in writing and read them line by line. The conditions that turn up:

  • The role or compensation changed after the start date. If you moved the target, cut the base, or reshaped the territory, an agreement can treat the placement as a different job, and the coverage lapses.
  • The manager changed. Some agreements void coverage if the person the hire reported to at the start is gone, on the reasoning that a new manager is a new environment.
  • Required onboarding steps were skipped. If the agreement calls for a ramp plan, training, or check-ins and those did not happen, the firm can argue the hire was set up to fail on your side.
  • The invoice is unpaid. An agreement can make coverage contingent on the fee being settled. A late or disputed payment can suspend it.
  • A notification window was missed. A guarantee can require you to report a departure inside a set number of days. Miss it and the claim can be denied.

None of these is unreasonable on its own. The problem is finding out about them after a hire has already fallen through. When a placement fails and the guarantee does not cover it, you carry the full cost of a bad sales hire: the open role, the lost pipeline, and the months it takes to start over. Get the full list before you sign, so the guarantee you are counting on is the one written in the contract.

What a guarantee does not do

A replacement guarantee returns a search. If you need the fee back, a refund guarantee is a different instrument, and the three-type table above is where to check which one you have been sold.

The re-run count is finite. Revenue Bench provides one, stated in the agreement, and a buyer should get that number from any firm in writing.

The window runs 90 days from the hire's first day. A guarantee that starts at the signed offer covers less calendar than one that starts on day one, which is why the start date is one of the seven terms below. Whether a replacement placement starts its own guarantee period is a separate term set in the agreement, so confirm it with any firm, including this one, before you sign.

No guarantee returns the months. A re-run replaces the search. It does not replace the quarter the open role cost you. DePaul's 2011-2012 Sales Effectiveness Survey of 435 B2B sales organizations puts the fully loaded cost of sales turnover at about $115,000, and Revenue Bench's own working figure for a revenue-role mis-hire is $200,000 to $250,000. The pipeline that did not get built sits on top of either number. The figure argues for getting selection right the first time rather than extending the window. If a hire is failing and you are weighing the re-run, this guide explains what to do when a sales hire is not working out.

What the guarantee is worth under contingency, container, and retained search

A placement guarantee is triggered by a placement, so its remedy answers one failure: a hire who started and did not last. Whether that trigger is the accepted offer or the hire's first day is set in the agreement, and the difference decides whether a candidate who accepts and never starts is covered. When that happens, the full fee has fallen due under all three fee models, and the guarantee is the term the buyer holds under each. The difference between the models sits in the failure the guarantee never reaches.

What a placement guarantee reaches under each recruiting fee model, and the failure it does not reach.
Fee model When the fee falls due A hire starts and fails inside the guarantee window The search produces no hire
Contingency Only when a candidate is hired and starts. The full fee has fallen due. The guarantee's remedy applies. No fee has fallen due, so no fee is at risk. The cost of the open role still sits with the buyer.
Container or engaged A fixed engagement fee up front, the balance when the hire starts. The full fee has fallen due. The guarantee's remedy applies. The engagement fee has fallen due. Where the guarantee is triggered by a hire who starts, it does not reach this outcome.
Retained In stages across the search, often in three stages such as kickoff, shortlist, and placement, whether or not a hire is made. The full fee has fallen due. The guarantee's remedy applies. The stages already invoiced stay due. Where the guarantee is triggered by a hire who starts, it does not reach this outcome.

A search that produces no hire is covered by a different term of the agreement. The guarantee does not reach it. This section is Revenue Bench's own structural read of how the fee models work and carries no external statistic. Before the first stage is invoiced, get in writing what happens to the stages already paid if the search does not produce a hire you want to make: whether they are credited to a re-scoped search, refunded, or kept. That answer belongs in the agreement alongside the guarantee terms, and the two cover different failures.

Revenue Bench runs senior and confidential searches, including CRO and VP of Sales roles, on the container or engaged model in the second row, which it describes as an engaged, retained-style search, and can run individual-contributor roles on contingency. The published 90-day guarantee covers a placement under either model. What happens to an engaged search that does not produce a hire is agreed before the search begins and is not published on this site, so ask Revenue Bench for it in writing on the same terms as any other firm. The models are set out in how we work with employers.

Candidate retention rates: what the number counts

A guarantee is the remedy a buyer holds when a hire fails inside a window. A candidate retention rate answers a further question, how often the firm's hires stay in the role at all. Anyone weighing a longer commitment usually wants both, and the retention rate is the harder to check.

Revenue Bench traced a one-year placement-success rate for this site between August and September 2026 and left it unpublished because the trail ended without a study, a stated sample, or a collection period. The work is recorded in the provenance index.

The one public search-performance dataset carrying a stated sample and period is Clockwork Recruiting's Executive Search Performance Benchmark Report. Published in October 2018, it covers 23,970 anonymized search projects run January 2015 to August 2018 and filtered to retained projects only, from Clockwork's own customer base of search firms using its applicant tracking system. It measures whether a search produced a hire. It reports nothing on how long a hire stayed. A retention rate shown to a buyer is therefore that firm's own figure, computed to that firm's own definitions, against no external standard.

The definitions ladder below is an illustration with invented figures and no Revenue Bench data. It shows how five defensible readings of one placement record produce five rates.

Take 20 placements, all made at least 24 months ago. Over those 24 months, 3 people resigned, 1 of them inside the first 12 months. Terminations for performance accounted for 2, 1 of them inside the first 12 months. A company-wide restructure cut 1. Another was promoted out of the sales role into a different function. The last was let go inside the guarantee window, the firm ran the search again at no additional fee, and that replacement is still in the role.

Departures other than a restructure or an internal move The 1 restructure and the 1 promotion into another function, on the reasoning that neither reflects selection 14 of 20, or 70%

Every figure in the table above is invented for this illustration and describes no firm's placements, Revenue Bench's included.

The definitions decide the answer, so ask for them before the number. A rate that cannot be traced back to a system of record cannot be checked by a buyer. The six questions below turn a quoted rate into one a buyer can check.

  • The population. Which placements are in the count: every placement, or one role band, one client type, or one period.
  • The window. Twelve months or twenty-four, and the date the window runs from.
  • The denominator. Every placement made, or only the ones the firm could follow up on.
  • What counts as a departure. A resignation, a termination for performance, a restructure, and a move into another function are four different events, and a rate can count or exclude each one.
  • How guarantee re-runs are treated. Whether a replaced hire counts once against the rate, twice, or not at all.
  • Who can verify it. Whether anything behind the number can be checked by a buyer, or whether it has to be taken on the firm's word.

A firm that keeps the underlying records can answer all six, and those answers are what make a percentage readable.

Revenue Bench does not publish a candidate retention rate. The firm opened in June 2026, so no Revenue Bench placement has been in role for twelve months. The track record carries this as its own row, and that row changes when a placement reaches twelve months in role. Revenue Bench provides a written count by role type on request. Before you commit to a multi-year agreement

A multi-year agreement applies its terms to hires that have yet to be briefed. The guarantee then works as the standing terms for everything the firm delivers across the agreement, which is why it belongs in the negotiation before the first search is scoped.

  • Guarantee terms. Get in writing whether the terms hold for the full agreement or reset at renewal.
  • Scope of cover. Ask whether the guarantee covers every placement, or is capped across the term as a pool.
  • Work in progress. Settle what happens to a search already running when the agreement ends.
  • Notice and termination. The notice period belongs in the agreement, along with whether either side can end it without cause.
  • Fee basis. Establish what the fee is fixed to, and which events allow it to change during the term.
  • Minimum search volume. Check whether the commitment carries a minimum number of searches, and what happens to any that go unused.
  • Recruiting restriction. Find out how long the firm agrees not to recruit out of your company, and whether that obligation survives the term.
  • Placement reporting. Agree what the firm reports on placements it has already made, and how often.

What a firm reports on placements it has already made is where the retention question becomes contractual. A buyer signing for a term can require the report itself: the placements made, the dates they started, and who is still in the role, on a stated cadence, with the definitions written into the agreement. Use the six questions above for those definitions.

Revenue Bench's fee is one clear percentage of the hire's first-year compensation, backed by the guarantee. The guarantee terms apply per placement and are published on this page. The full terms are agreed before the search begins. Senior and confidential searches, including CRO and VP of Sales roles, run as an engaged, retained-style search. Individual-contributor roles can run on contingency. Read how we work with employers and recruiter fee structures for those published details.

Where these figures come from

About these figures

The market figures come from Top Echelon's survey of members of its recruiting network, published in March 2019. Its respondents are independent third-party recruiters in that network, and corporate recruiters were excluded. Two limits go with the figures. Top Echelon does not publish how many recruiters responded, so the distribution carries no sample size. The survey also does not separate retained search from contingency search, so it cannot be read as a description of retained executive search practice. It is the only public survey we have found that quantifies both guarantee length and remedy type. On length: 90 days accounted for 44.9%, 30 days for 20.3%, 60 days for 20.0%, other windows for 5.7%, six months for 2.4%, one year for 2.3%, 45 days for 1.9%, and no response for 2.5%. On remedy: replacement with no money back accounted for 61.4%, a prorated refund for 17.6%, other remedies for 10.9%, a full refund for 8.4%, and no response for 1.7%. Both sets are published as complete distributions here because a partial list of categories reads as a whole one. Those are Top Echelon's survey figures, and because the survey dates from 2019, they describe a market norm rather than a current census. We have found no public survey that has updated them since, so a buyer comparing guarantee terms in 2026 is comparing against a 2019 baseline.

The coaching figures are OMG's own data. Frequently asked

What is a placement guarantee in recruitment?

A placement guarantee is the recruiter's written commitment covering what happens if a hire leaves or is let go inside a defined window after the start date. It takes one of three forms: a free replacement search, a refund of some or all of the fee, or a credit toward a future search. The form and the conditions that void it matter more than the length of the window.

What is the standard placement guarantee period?

In Top Echelon's 2019 survey of its recruiting-network members, 90 days was the most common window at 44.9%. Thirty days accounted for 20.3%, 60 days for 20.0%, six months for 2.4%, and one year for 2.3%. The survey does not separate retained search from contingency search, and it publishes no respondent count. The conditions attached to a window decide what it covers, so read them alongside the number of days.

How many recruiting firms offer a one-year guarantee?

In Top Echelon's 2019 survey of its recruiting-network members, 2.3% of firms used a one-year window and 2.4% used six months, while 90 days at 44.9% was the market default. That survey is the only public source we have found that quantifies guarantee length, it publishes no respondent count, and it dates from 2019, so it describes a norm rather than a current census.

Do recruiters offer a replacement or a refund?

In Top Echelon's 2019 survey, replacement with no money back accounted for 61.4%, a prorated refund for 17.6%, other remedies for 10.9%, a full refund for 8.4%, and no response for 1.7%. The survey publishes no respondent count. Revenue Bench's published remedy is a replacement search with one free re-run and no conditions.

Is a 12-month placement guarantee better than a 90-day guarantee?

Inside the first 90 days, a guarantee that excludes nothing covers more situations than a 12-month guarantee that excludes some. From day 91 through month 12, the longer guarantee covers whatever its exclusions permit, while the 90-day guarantee covers nothing. Compare the seven terms of each agreement rather than the two numbers.

When does the guarantee window start?

It depends on the agreement, and the difference matters. Some guarantees start the clock on the signed offer, others on the candidate's first day of work. Those can be weeks apart. Ask which date your guarantee uses and get it in writing, because a window that starts at signing gives you less coverage than one that starts on day one.

What voids a placement guarantee?

Common void conditions include a change to the role or compensation after the start date, a change of manager, skipped onboarding steps the agreement required, an unpaid invoice, and a missed notification window for reporting the departure. None is unusual, but many firms keep the list in the contract rather than the marketing. Ask for the full list in writing before you sign.

Do search firms publish their placement guarantee terms?

Mostly they do not. Top Echelon's 2019 survey of recruiters in its own network found that 95.9% offered a guarantee, which records what those firms said they offered. In September 2026 Revenue Bench read the public websites of 28 sales and executive search firms, reaching 26 of them. Among those 26, 11 (42%) stated that a guarantee exists, 6 (23%) stated a period, and 1 (4%) stated when the clock starts. The sweep measures website disclosure alone, so a buyer has to ask a firm directly.

What should I expect from a search firm after the executive starts?

A guarantee and post-placement support answer two different questions. The guarantee sets the remedy when a hire fails. Post-placement support is the work the firm does inside that window, before any claim arises. Revenue Bench provides a two-day sales skills program for reps or a leadership refresher for leaders at or before day one, plus a dedicated onboarding coach who meets the hire weekly and the hiring manager weekly for the first 90 days, reports to the hiring manager, and runs over the same 90 days the replacement guarantee covers.

What happens when a placement guarantee is used?

Under Revenue Bench's published terms, the client decides the hire is not working inside the first 90 days from the hire's start date and tells Revenue Bench the guarantee is being used. Revenue Bench revisits the written success profile and runs the search again at no additional fee. One free re-run applies, with no conditions, and whether the hire is working is the client's call.

How long does a replacement search take?

When the bench covers the role, kickoff to shortlist on the re-run is commonly 2 to 4 weeks, and a pure targeted search takes longer. Offer, acceptance, training, and the replacement hire's start follow the shortlist.

Does a placement guarantee refund the fee?

A replacement guarantee returns a search rather than money. Under Revenue Bench's published terms, we run the search again at no additional fee, with one free re-run and no conditions. A fee refund is a different guarantee type, so buyers should confirm the remedy in writing.

How many times will a recruiter re-run a search?

The agreement sets the number of re-runs, and buyers should get that number in writing before signing. Revenue Bench's standard agreement provides one free re-run at no additional fee, with no conditions. Market practice varies by firm.

What is a good candidate retention rate for a recruiting firm?

No public benchmark exists. Revenue Bench traced a one-year placement-success rate in 2026 and found no source carrying a stated sample and collection period, so any rate shown to a buyer is that firm's own figure under that firm's own definitions. The same placement record can produce different rates depending on the window, the denominator, and what counts as a departure. Ask for the definitions before the number.

What should I check before signing a multi-year recruitment agreement?

Check whether the guarantee is fixed for the term, whether it applies per placement or is pooled, notice and termination, what fixes the fee, and what the firm reports on placements already made. On a longer term a buyer can require that reporting in the agreement, with the definitions written in, so the figures come from the record. Revenue Bench's published record answers two of those checks, and the rest sit in the agreement.

Revenue Bench provides a written count by role type on request and offers a client introduction with the placement's start date during an evaluation. What guarantees do retained executive search firms typically offer?

No public survey Revenue Bench has found quantifies guarantee terms in retained executive search. The one public survey that quantifies guarantee length and remedy is Top Echelon's, published in March 2019. Its respondents are independent third-party recruiters in one network, it publishes no respondent count, and it does not separate retained search from contingency search. A retained buyer has no published norm to compare against and should compare the terms of the specific agreement: the window and its start date, the remedy, and the void conditions.

Does a placement guarantee cover a search that produces no hire?

No. A placement guarantee is triggered by a placement, so a no-hire search sits outside it whatever name the agreement gives that clause. Under contingency, a search that produces no hire costs the buyer nothing because the fee falls due only when a hire starts. Under container or retained search, the buyer has already paid for that search and the placement guarantee does not reach it. The agreement should therefore state how stages already invoiced will be treated.
