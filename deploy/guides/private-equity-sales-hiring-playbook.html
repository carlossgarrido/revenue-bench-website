The private equity operating partner's sales-hiring playbook

By Carlos Garrido, Co-Founder, Revenue Bench10 min read

The value-creation math moved from multiples to operations. Bain's Global Private Equity Report 2026 puts numbers on the shift. A 2015 deal could reach a 2.5x with roughly 5% annual EBITDA growth, because multiples and leverage covered the rest. The same return today needs 10% to 12%, year after year, from operations. The connection to sales hiring is Revenue Bench's argument: the sales team is an operating lever a fund can govern with evidence, seat by seat. This playbook is the governance model for assessing, hiring, and building revenue talent across the hold.

The thesis: govern sales hiring like a value-creation workstream

Without a portfolio standard, each portfolio company hires to its own bar. The portfolio collects local decisions with different hiring bars, different evidence, and different definitions of success. The operating partner sees the outcome after the seat has shaped the plan.

This playbook puts evidence at each hold phase. It gives the deal team a pre-close view of revenue durability, gives the portco CEO a sequence for leadership and hiring decisions, and gives the operating partner a portfolio-wide standard that can be audited through exit preparation. The failure side of the same workstream is set out in the sales-hiring mistakes deal teams repeat, which pairs each mistake with the event where its cost surfaces.

Phase one: diligence

Pre-close sales diligence begins with the leader and the sellers who carry the plan. Assess the sales leader's ability to set standards, coach the team, hold people accountable, and translate the investment thesis into a hiring profile. Assess the top sellers for role fit, selling capability, and dependence on founder relationships. The goal is a credible view of who can perform under the ownership plan and where the organization already carries execution risk.

The data room answers questions about bookings, concentration, pipeline, territories, compensation, and turnover. It shows what the sales organization has produced. A sales-specific assessment answers a different set of questions. It shows whether the leader can build the team the plan requires, whether top performers can repeat their success under a new motion, and whether apparent underperformance comes from capability, role fit, or management. Sales team due diligence for private equity buyers carries the complete pre-LOI request list and the red flags for each area. Who provides the sales-team assessment in private equity due diligence sets out Revenue Bench's role between the LOI and close and how the assessment differs from the quota-attainment record. The guide's seller-concentration exposure calculator runs the exposure arithmetic on the target's own figures. Its six pipeline and sales-process tests set out what the data room can show about pipeline quality before anyone meets the team. Where the deal team needs outside help, the guide's table of sales due diligence specialists sets each diligence question beside who can answer it.

That baseline becomes useful after close. It supports diagnosing a struggling sales hire before the company defaults to another search. Revenue Bench's assessment-led process carries the same evidence standard from role definition through selection and coached onboarding.

Phase two: the first 100 days

The first seat decision belongs at the manager level. The sales leader defines the operating cadence, the hiring bar, and the standard every seller will experience. A company that starts with rep replacements while leaving an unfit manager in place asks new hires to enter the same system that weakened the inherited team. Whether the operating layer around those seats needs a change is a separate question, taken up in the revenue operations layer in a portfolio company. Who supplies that layer, once the company has decided it needs one, is mapped in the supply routes for a portfolio company. The routes a fund uses to fill the sales leader's role itself are compared in the six routes to a portfolio company's revenue leader.

The promotion path needs the same scrutiny. In OMG's evaluation of 44,493 sales managers, only 9% were strong in all three coaching qualities. In the same data, managers strong in all three have 80% more elite salespeople on their teams. The comparison is between managers who hold all three qualities and managers who do not, so it does not show the qualities as the cause. That supports assessing management capacity before a strong seller moves into the role. The full decision framework appears in Should you promote your best rep into sales management?

Once the manager decision is settled, sequence seat changes around the selling motion and the value-creation plan. Each replacement should enter under written guarantee terms and a coached onboarding plan. Under Revenue Bench's terms, the onboarding coach meets the hire and the hiring manager weekly for the first 90 days, reports to the hiring manager, and exists to give a read on the hire's fit and aptitude inside that window, so a mis-hire is identified early and its impact contained. These moves start operating work that continues across the hold, and the build phase carries it forward through the quarterly audit below. The window-by-window order, the evidence each decision rests on, and the owner accountable for closing it appear in the first 100 days: rebuilding a portfolio company's sales team.

What an operating partner audits in sales hiring each quarter

Each quarter, a private equity operating partner should audit six things in each portfolio company's sales hiring: role definitions, assessments before interviews, manager promotions, onboarding, first-year departures, and guarantee windows. The audit reads whether the evidence exists and when it was produced, so it can be run from documents without re-running any search. The portfolio company CEO owns the result, consistent with the build phase of the governance model above, and the table names who closes each gap.

The quarterly sales-hiring audit for a portfolio company: each audit item, the evidence to request, what a gap looks like, and who closes it
Audit itemThe evidence to requestWhat a gap looks likeWho closes it
Role definition A written success profile for every seat opened this quarter, dated before the search opened. The profile was written after the shortlist or copied from an old job post. The hiring manager
Assessment before interviews For every seller and sales-leader hire this quarter, a sales-specific assessment completed before the first interview. The assessment ran after the offer or was skipped for a referral or an internal candidate. The portco CEO
Manager promotions For any seller promoted into management this quarter, the management-capacity evidence the decision rested on. The promotion rested on the seller's own results alone. The portco CEO
Onboarding plans For every hire inside the first 90 days, a written ramp plan with dated gates and a named owner reporting progress to the hiring manager. There are no written gates, or progress is reported only when the hire is already missing the target. The hiring manager
First-year departures Every hire who left within the first year, by employee ID, with the departure date, the recorded reason, and whether selection evidence exists for the hire. Departures are recorded without the selection evidence, so the next search cannot test whether selection played a part. The portco CEO
Guarantee windows For every placement still inside a guarantee window, the window's start date, end date, and remedy in writing. A window ends before anyone has checked the hire against it. The hiring manager

Individual assessment results, departure reasons, and other employee records stay with the portfolio company under its own confidentiality and data-handling policy, and reach the operating partner only as that policy allows.

The turnover arithmetic across a portfolio

A portfolio carries the turnover cost of every company in it. In OMG's 2024 validation survey, first-year turnover ran 9% for hires the assessment recommended and 33% for candidates it advised against. The comparison describes selection: it sets the two groups side by side and does not measure what an assessment changes in any one hire.

DePaul University's Center for Sales Leadership, in its 2011-2012 Sales Effectiveness Survey of 435 B2B sales organizations, puts the average cost of sales turnover near $49,508 and close to $115,000 fully loaded. An operating partner can count first-year departures across the portfolio's quota-carrying headcount using row 5 of the quarterly audit, price each departure at the company's own replacement cost with DePaul's figures as a reference point, and set the total beside the value-creation plan.

In OMG's 2024 validation survey, 72% of hires the assessment recommended reached the top half of their sales force within 12 months. The portfolio standard records whether each candidate was assessed before the first interview.

What to demand from any search partner, portfolio-wide

A portfolio search standard starts with assessment evidence before interviews. Every candidate should be measured against the selling motion and the role's requirements before personal chemistry shapes the process. The operating partner should be able to see the recommendation, the role-fit evidence, and the reason each candidate reached the shortlist, as the portfolio company's data-handling policy allows.

Guarantee terms belong in writing: the start date, the remedy, the conditions, and who decides. Revenue Bench's published terms are a replacement guarantee measured from the hire's start date, 90 days for sellers and GTM roles and 6 to 12 months for sales leaders, under which Revenue Bench keeps replacing at no additional fee until a hire works out. Every placement includes a weekly onboarding coach who reports to the hiring manager. The complete terms appear in the seven placement guarantee terms, compared.

The program also needs one accountable operator. That person owns role definition, assessment quality, candidate presentation, and the handoff into onboarding. Leadership searches require an explicit scorecard for the commercial system the person will inherit. Revenue Bench's VP of Sales hiring guide shows how that standard applies to the portfolio's highest-consequence revenue seat. This is the standard behind how Revenue Bench recruits revenue leaders for PE-backed portfolio companies.

Frequently asked

What should a PE operating partner audit in a portfolio company's sales hiring each quarter?

A PE operating partner should audit role definitions, assessments before interviews, manager promotions, onboarding, first-year departures, and guarantee windows each quarter. The audit reads whether the evidence exists and when it was produced. The portco CEO owns the result, and the hiring manager closes the gaps in role definitions, onboarding, and guarantee windows. Individual assessment results and departure reasons stay with the portfolio company under its data-handling policy.

How do private equity firms recruit sales leaders for portfolio companies?

Private equity firms recruit sales leaders by defining the value-creation work the role must own, then assessing candidates against that operating mandate before interviews begin. The process should test sales leadership, coaching capacity, hiring judgment, accountability, and fit with the company's selling motion. An operating partner sets the portfolio standard, while the portco CEO owns the final working relationship and the leader's performance after the hire.

What should a PE firm assess in a portfolio company's sales team before closing?

A PE firm should assess the sales leader, top sellers, role fit, coaching strength, and dependence on founder relationships. The data room can show revenue history, pipeline, concentration, compensation, and turnover. A sales-specific assessment shows whether the people behind those results can perform under the new plan. Together, those views reveal leadership gaps, vulnerable seats, and the sequence of talent decisions that should follow close.

When should a PE firm replace a portfolio company's VP of Sales?

A PE firm should make the decision after assessing the leader against the future operating mandate. Evidence should cover the leader's ability to coach, recruit, set standards, hold the team accountable, and build the sales organization the investment plan requires. Replacement becomes appropriate when the assessment and operating evidence show a material gap that coaching or role clarification cannot close within the company's value-creation plan. The keep, coach or replace decision for an inherited sales leader sets out the evidence for each branch. Revenue Bench is paid when a leader is replaced, so publishing the conditions under which the right answer is to keep the leader runs against that interest.

What does OMG's outcome data show for assessment-led sales hiring across a portfolio?

Assessment-led hiring gives operating partners a consistent selection bar across portfolio companies. OMG's 2024 validation survey found first-year turnover of 9% for recommended hires and 33% for candidates it advised against, and 72% of recommended hires reached the top half of their sales force within 12 months. The figures compare candidates the assessment recommended with candidates it advised against, so they describe selection and do not measure an effect on any one hire. The arithmetic for a portfolio runs on its own first-year departures and replacement cost.

What guarantee should a PE firm demand from a sales recruiter?

A PE firm should demand written terms that identify the coverage window, its start date, the remedy, every condition, and who decides whether the hire is working. Revenue Bench publishes a replacement guarantee measured from the hire's start date, 90 days for sellers and GTM roles and 6 to 12 months for sales leaders, and keeps replacing at no additional fee until a hire works out. Every placement also includes a weekly onboarding coach who reports to the hiring manager.
