Sales compensation benchmarks for 2026

By Carlos Garrido, Co-Founder, Revenue Bench9 min read

Every sales compensation plan rests on two numbers: the market rate for the role and what the company can afford per dollar of new revenue. This guide shows where each number comes from and how to build the plan from them. The page does not publish a salary table because a benchmark without a source, a sample, and a date is decoration. Sales-leader packages in sponsor-owned companies are a different market with their own guide.

Key takeaways

  • Anchor every plan on two numbers: the market rate for the role and total sales cost as a share of new revenue. Revenue Bench's working range is 15 to 25 percent.
  • Derive the market number from sourced data and your own offer history rather than a published table. A live search is the freshest read of what the role costs.
  • Match the base-variable mix to what the person directly controls.
  • Set quota the median performer can meaningfully reach, and pay accelerators above 100 percent of plan with no cap.

The two anchors

The market rate tells you what a qualified person can command for the role you have defined. Against that sits affordability: how much the selling model can carry for each dollar of new revenue. Revenue Bench's working range for total sales cost, meaning fully loaded selling expense across the team, is 15 to 25 percent of new revenue.

The tension between those anchors is the design job: pay enough to win the person the role needs, inside a cost the model can sustain. The mix, quota, and payout mechanics need to fit inside the overlap.

Where the market number comes from

Use three sources, moving from broad published direction toward evidence from the live role.

  1. Published compensation guides from recruiters and compensation-data platforms. They offer useful direction, but each reflects its publisher's client base, sample, and cutoff date. Figures vary widely between sources, and a table without those three things attached cannot price your role.
  2. The company's offer-and-decline history. What candidates accepted, declined, and countered over the last year is benchmark data nobody else has. It reveals where the company's role, story, and offer meet the market.
  3. The live market during a search. Qualified candidates in your geography and selling motion show what they will accept this quarter. This is the freshest benchmark there is, and it arrives as a byproduct of running the search itself. The company reads that evidence and sets its own number.

Offer history and live candidate response show what the market cleared. The role's range is still built from job-related criteria: scope, selling motion, and plan responsibility. Hold the same range for every candidate considered, and disclose it, at minimum where the jurisdiction requires disclosure. A candidate's current or prior compensation does not enter the calculation; salary-history inquiries are restricted in numerous US states and cities, and counsel should confirm the requirements that apply to the search.

That is why this page teaches the method instead of printing a table: the table a buyer needs is the one built from their own plan, and the next section builds it.

Decision artifact #1: The plan ledger, worked

The plan ledger builds the affordability anchor from the company's own numbers. Start with planned new revenue, choose a point inside the working cost range, and test whether headcount, pay, quota, margin, and sales-cycle assumptions hold together.

1 What new revenue is planned for the year? The company's own plan $3,000,000
2 What can selling cost? $3,000,000 x 20 percent, the midpoint of the 15-to-25 working range $600,000 fully loaded

The ledger is one worked illustration on stated assumptions. Every dollar figure in it exists to show the arithmetic, and none of them is a market benchmark. Most companies also assign total quota above the revenue target so the plan absorbs misses; the illustration keeps the two equal for clarity.

Decision artifact #2: The role-design table

The base-variable mix is a design decision that follows what the person directly controls. The decision requires no dollar figures.

Role-design table for setting base-variable mix by direct control
Role What the person directly controls Where the mix leans The failure pattern when it is set wrong
SDR / BDR Activity volume and meeting quality, rather than deal outcomes Base-heavy, with variable pay on qualified meetings held Paying on booked-but-not-held meetings buys calendar spam.
AE, new-logo The deal from first meeting to close The loosest mix on the team, approaching an even split as deal control rises A base-heavy plan pays for effort while the pipeline stalls.
AE / AM, retention-heavy Shared outcomes with product, service, and success teams Base-heavy, with variable pay on retention plus expansion A hunter mix on a farmer role pays for churn nobody caused and rewards discounting to hold logos.
Front-line sales manager Coaching, pipeline discipline, and hiring, rather than individual deals Base-heavy, with variable pay on team attainment A manager paid on personal deals competes with their own reps.
VP / Head of Sales The system: process, hiring, and forecast Base-heavy, with variable pay on the operating plan the board reviews An all-commission leader optimizes this quarter and starves next year's foundation.

Setting quota

The common failure is a number only the star reaches. It looks ambitious in a plan and demoralizes everyone else when sustained effort has little chance of producing meaningful payout.

Revenue Bench's working test is that the median performer can meaningfully reach 70 to 80 percent of plan. That standard protects motivation because effort visibly pays. It also improves forecast honesty, since a plan most of the team will miss overstates the pipeline.

Accelerators, draws, and clawbacks

Above 100 percent of plan, the rate on the overage rises and is never capped. A cap prices the marginal effort of the exact people who can carry the number at zero and gives them a stopping point.

A draw can bridge a new hire through ramp while the person builds a working pipeline. State in writing whether the draw is recoverable, and have counsel review the wage terms of a recoverable draw before offering it.

A clawback recovers paid commission when revenue reverses. Define the trigger events and the look-back window in the plan document, then have counsel review those terms as well. Clear recovery rules connect payout to revenue quality without leaving either party to interpret the plan after a reversal.

What the plan cannot do

One of Carlos Garrido's operating philosophies reads, "Your compensation model shapes your sales behavior and outcome." The plan steers the people you have; every plan encourages some behaviors and discourages others, so the design question is which.

OMG's data shows most salespeople are intrinsically motivated, and only about a quarter are extrinsically motivated. A richer plan alone will not move most of a team, and pay cannot install drive that did not arrive with the person. Treat drive as a hiring decision.

Methodology and sources

The working ranges on this page, the 15-to-25-percent envelope and the quota test, are Revenue Bench's own working guidance from sales hiring and selection work rather than survey findings. The OMG figure is OMG's research.

Frequently asked

How much should I pay a salesperson in 2026?

Anchor on two numbers: the market rate for the role, from sourced compensation data, your own offer history, and live candidate response, and affordability, using Revenue Bench's working range of 15 to 25 percent of new revenue as total sales cost. A single published dollar figure cannot price your role without your geography, motion, and deal size attached.

What is a good base-to-variable mix for sales roles?

The mix follows what the person directly controls. New-logo roles run the loosest mix on the team; retention, management, and leadership roles carry a higher base share.

How should I set sales quota?

Revenue Bench's working test is that the median performer can meaningfully reach 70 to 80 percent of plan. That level keeps effort visibly connected to pay and keeps the forecast honest, because a plan most of the team will miss overstates the pipeline.

Should I cap sales commission?

No. Pay accelerators above 100 percent of plan. A cap prices the marginal effort of your best performers at zero and tells them when to stop selling.

Where can I find sales compensation benchmark data?

Use published guides from recruiters and compensation platforms for direction, with their sample and date caveats attached, then add your own offer-and-decline history. The live market during a search is the freshest read of what a role costs this quarter.
