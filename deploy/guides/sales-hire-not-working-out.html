How Long Should You Give a Sales Hire Who Is Not Working Out?

By Carlos Garrido, Co-Founder, Revenue Bench 18 min read

Before a company may judge a hire, it has to show that the hire was managed. The day-90 evidence can identify a weak profile, a missing process, failed onboarding, a changed role, or a broken operating system. Its meaning depends on whether the company supplied the job definition, manager, ramp, process, coaching, working conditions, and usable feedback that the person was owed.

The answer first

Revenue Bench reads a struggling sales hire at day 90, on booked meetings, conversations that reach a second step, and behavior that changes after coaching. That is this firm's own position from search work, and no study we could find fixes the point at which the evidence becomes readable. Whether the company may act on what it sees depends on a prior check. The hire must have been managed, and the record can show whether that happened. Revenue Bench is paid when a company runs a search, so we publish an audit that can tell the company to stop.

Key takeaways

  • The day-90 read usually supplies enough leading evidence to diagnose the problem. A closed number this early remains a lagging measure.
  • The company is audited first. Three or more missing management conditions mean the company has an unmanaged hire without a usable performance case.
  • Five failures can produce the same weak output. Wrong profile is a selection decision; process, onboarding, a changed role, and the operating system are fixable.
  • A hire's response to visible accountability is an early behavioral signal. It informs the diagnosis without settling it.
  • Coaching, replacement, and open-ended waiting carry different costs. The territory determines the largest variable in each branch.

What the day-90 read shows

The market's common answer is six to nine months, when a complete sales cycle has run and the manager feels able to call the ramp. Revenue Bench reads it earlier, at day 90. That position comes from search work. We looked for a study identifying when the evidence on a new seller becomes readable and found none with a stated sample and collection period. What day 90 rests on is which signals mature by then. By that point the record shows whether the seller books qualified meetings, whether conversations reach a second step, and whether coaching changes behavior. Those are readable leading indicators. A closed number this early is a lagging measure and tells you less about what the seller will produce once the pipeline matures.

Giving a struggling hire until month nine gets framed as patience. It reads more like avoidance when the leading indicators have stayed flat and the manager has already seen the pattern. The day-90 read marks the point where this firm reads the evidence. It does not create a dismissal deadline, set a standard the company owes anyone, or determine the length of any improvement period. Where any of this bears on ending someone's employment, employment counsel decides the timing and the record.

That read has value only when the first ninety days contained the conditions the hire was owed. A company that supplied an unclear job, irregular management, or no working process cannot treat the resulting output as clean evidence about the person.

Was this hire given a fair test?

The employer's record comes before the seller's result. Each line below is either delivered or undelivered, based on evidence the company can produce.

A seven-part audit of whether the company managed a struggling sales hire
What the company owed the hire What it looks like when it was delivered What it looks like when it was not What its absence means for the decision
A written definition of the job. The hire can state, without looking it up, what they are measured on and by when. The target appeared in the interview and was never written down, or it changed after the start date without being restated. Underperformance against an unstated target does not create a documented performance case.
A named manager who owns them. One person is accountable for the hire's ramp, and both people know who carries that responsibility. The hire reports to a founder who is also selling, reports to two people, or lost the reporting line when the role turned over during ramp. The reporting line must be examined before the failure can be attributed to the person.
A sales process they could follow. Written stages, a qualification standard, and a shared definition of what moves a deal forward. Every seller runs an individual method. This is the no-process failure in the next table, and the company owns it.
A ramp with dates on it. A documented first 90 days states what happens in each phase. The sales onboarding plan shows the required structure. The company supplied a laptop, quota, and start date. The hire never received the conditions the ramp was meant to create, so the clock has not honestly started.
Coaching on a held cadence. A recurring one-to-one occurred, deals were reviewed, and behavior was corrected. The meeting was scheduled and moved, or it became a forecast call. The largest single management input to the seller's performance was never applied. The OMG coaching figures below show what that input is worth.
Territory, list, and pipeline the job assumed. The accounts, leads, or inbound flow described at offer stage arrived. The territory was reassigned, leads did not arrive, or the product moved. The job accepted by the person differs from the job they hold, so comparison with the original plan is invalid.
Feedback the hire could act on, in writing, before now. At least one documented conversation named the gap and described what closing it would look like. The first notice of a problem arrives during the conversation about the hire's future. The record is absent. The resulting legal and human exposure belongs with employment counsel.

How to read it. All seven delivered means the company has a performance case, and the next table identifies its type. With one or two missing, close the gaps and set a defined observation window before reading the result again. At three or more missing, you do not have a performance case, you have an unmanaged hire. The decision waits. Replacement under those conditions repeats the result with a new name and becomes the most expensive way to learn that the environment caused the failure.

Row 7 is read separately. Its absence leaves the diagnosis unchanged and changes who must join the conversation. Route the situation to employment counsel.

The same standard has to reach everyone at the same level

Where one seller enters a documented improvement window and another at a similar level of attainment does not, the difference needs a reason the company can state. Whether that reason holds is a question for employment counsel, and this page does not answer it.

Some situations cannot be scored on performance

Where a hire has recently raised a concern, taken leave, or reported something, the situation changes character and the audit above no longer applies. Counsel should review the circumstances before the audit is used. A clean result from the instrument does not make the situation clean.

This audit can conclude that the company is the problem, and Revenue Bench publishes it knowing that. Where it does, the work is not a search and there is nothing to buy here. Close the missing conditions first: the 90-day onboarding plan supplies the ramp and coaching cadence, and where the operating system is the gap, the revenue operations and enablement layer covers what has to exist. Then read the hire again.

The five ways a sales hire fails

Once the employer passes the audit, weak output can be sorted into five causes. The broader guide to the root causes of failed sales hires covers the hiring conditions behind them.

Five failure types for a struggling sales hire
Failure What you see The check that separates it from the others What fixing it costs Fixable?
Wrong profile. Low activity stays low. Coaching lands for a day and fades. The drive to pursue a deal through rejection is absent. Run an objective, sales-specific assessment of the core drivers. The salesperson assessment guide explains the instrument. In OMG's data, a rep's Will to Sell moves only 0% to 6% after training. A replacement search and a second ramp. No. This is a selection decision.
No process. A capable, motivated person improvises every deal. Could this person follow a written sales process if one existed, or are they inventing one alone every week? The work of writing and teaching a process the team needs anyway. Yes.
No onboarding. A capable person received a laptop and a quota. Has anyone coached this hire on a held cadence? A structured ramp and the manager's weekly hours. Yes.
The role changed under them. The territory was reassigned, the ICP moved, the product shipped late, the compensation plan changed mid-year, or the expected inbound flow did not arrive. A competent person's numbers stopped tracking a plan that no longer describes the job. Read the offer-stage description against the role held today, line by line. If three or more items moved, the plan changed and the person did not. Restating the job and resetting the target from today. Yes, and quickly, when someone is willing to say the plan moved.
The system around the seat. No defined process, CRM discipline, or management cadence exists, and sellers have repeatedly arrived and left. The same failure appears across different people. How many people held the role in three years, and did any succeed? If none did, the role and its environment are the finding. The need may include revenue operations and enablement capacity. Fixing the operating system before another search runs. Yes. No recruiter can do it for you. Revenue Bench will say so and decline to run the search.

When the manager is part of the answer

Carlos Garrido's 11 Golden Rules of Sales Leadership states: “Accountability is alchemy, it turns lead into gold. Accountability is not punishment. It is the act of making performance visible and meaningful. High performers crave it. Low performers flee from it. Use that information.” How a struggling hire responds to being measured is readable before any assessment runs. A person who asks for the number, tracks it, and brings bad news early may be behaving like a high performer inside a bad situation. Someone who avoids the review supplies a different signal. It remains a signal and does not become a verdict.

OMG's study of 11,078 salespeople and their managers found that reps coached several times a week gain 17 percentile points, while about 10% of reps receive that cadence. These are OMG's figures. The frequency is higher than most teams run, so the direction is the useful point. Carlos Garrido's Golden Rhythms of Sales Leadership describes a cadence layered progressively from daily observation through weekly coaching, monthly operating review, and quarterly direction setting. Held over months, that cadence is what a company points to when it says the hire was managed.

The same leadership framework says: “The north star metric is time to profitability. If a new hire isn't profitable quickly, the team is sailing with the anchor down.” Ramp time is a revenue and culture measure as much as an HR one. That is why day 90 is the right point to read the leading evidence, while the final employment decision remains specific to the company and person.

The same framework states: “Clarity is kindness.” Waiting past readable evidence serves nobody. The manager's honest contribution is to state what good performance looks like, when it will be read, what support the company will deliver, and what consequence follows if the expectation is unmet.

The decision sequence, and when each step belongs

The sequence for deciding whether to coach or replace a struggling sales hire
Step When it belongs What it produces Who else is in it
Run the fair-test audit. Before any conversation about the person's future. Whether the company has a performance case at all. The hire's manager, honestly.
Close any gap the audit found. Immediately, before the clock is read. The conditions that make the company's defined observation window meaningful. Whoever owns the missing piece, usually someone other than the hire.
Read the leading indicators at day 90 from the hire's start date. When the early pipeline and coaching record can be read. Booked meetings, conversations that reach a second step, and whether coaching changes behavior. A closed number this early is a lagging measure and tells you less. The manager and hire together.
If the indicators are flat, run an objective, sales-specific assessment. After the company has passed the audit and the leading evidence remains weak. The answer to wrong profile against fixable, which is the question that controls the rest of the decision. See how to assess a salesperson and the comparison of a sales assessment with an interview. The assessment provider.
Set one defined window with written expectations, or make the decision. After the audit and assessment have separated a fixable failure from a selection decision. A documented improvement period the hire can act on, or a decision made on evidence. Employment counsel, before anything is communicated.
Rebuild the search, if that is the answer. After the employment decision is agreed with counsel. A role definition corrected for what the audit found and a screen that catches what the first process missed. Whoever runs the search.

A written improvement period is a documented performance conversation with a defined window and stated expectations, agreed with counsel. During that period, the company delivers the support it names and records that delivery alongside the expectation. Revenue Bench does not draft the document.

Deals in flight, commissions earned, and amounts owed at separation vary by state and contract. This page decides none of them.

If the answer is replace, what changes in the next search

The role definition is rewritten from the missing conditions in the fair-test audit. Territory, expected pipeline, reporting ownership, process maturity, and ramp support should describe the job that exists today. Recruiting against the old description would screen candidates for a role the company no longer has.

Whichever failure the triage identified becomes an explicit screen in the next process, and the screen differs by failure. Absent drivers mean the next search assesses for them before anyone reaches a final interview. A missing process or a missing ramp puts the burden on the company to produce evidence of both, alongside whatever it asks of the candidate. Where the role moved under the last hire, it enters the search at the scope it holds today. And a system failure stops the search until the operating conditions can support a hire.

Where the seat is a sales leadership role and the incumbent is still in it, the search runs under constraints of its own, set out in the guide to running a confidential replacement search. The assessment should reach every candidate at the same stage, ahead of finalist selection. Consistent placement in the process gives the company comparable evidence and prevents interview preference from deciding who receives objective scrutiny.

Conditions missing from the employer audit should exist before the next start date. The named manager, written ramp, coaching cadence, territory, and feedback process become search inputs because a second hire entering the same conditions is likely to produce the same finding.

Guarantee terms carry the cost of a second miss. Revenue Bench's published placement guarantee is a 90-day replacement guarantee running from the hire's start date, one free re-run of the search, no conditions, the client's call, plus a 90-day onboarding coach who meets the hire weekly and reports to the hiring manager. The same terms appear in the way Revenue Bench works with employers.

What this page does not decide

The page does not decide any individual's employment. That decision belongs with the company and its employment counsel.

It does not substitute for an assessment. The sequence tells the company when an objective, sales-specific assessment belongs in the decision.

A sales leader inherited through an acquisition presents a different decision. The leader-level guide for an inherited sales leader covers that case.

We looked for a study measuring how often a struggling sales hire recovers and found none with a stated sample and collection period. This page publishes no recovery rate. If a reader can point us to a qualifying study, we will carry it.

Frequently asked

Questions about a struggling sales hire, answered

Should I fire a struggling sales rep or coach them?

Audit the company's management of the hire first. If the company delivered the job definition, manager, process, ramp, coaching, working conditions, and written feedback, assess the seller's sales-specific drivers. Use that evidence to separate a fixable failure from a selection decision, then choose coaching or replacement. Employment counsel should review any documented improvement period or separation before it is communicated.

What should you look at in a struggling hire's first 90 days?

Look for booked meetings, conversations that reach a second step, and behavior that changes after coaching. Those leading indicators show whether the seller is creating credible movement before a complete sales cycle has matured. A closed number this early is a lagging measure and can conceal useful progress or make weak activity look temporarily acceptable. Read the activity, progression, and response to coaching together.

How do I know if the problem is the rep or the company?

Run the fair-test audit against the company. Check the written job definition, named manager, sales process, dated ramp, held coaching cadence, promised territory and pipeline, and prior written feedback. If three or more conditions were missing, you do not have a performance case, you have an unmanaged hire. Close the gaps and use a defined observation window before reading the seller again.

Can a bad sales hire be turned around?

The answer depends on the failure. In OMG's data, a rep's Will to Sell moves only 0% to 6% after training, so a wrong-profile result is a selection decision. Missing process, missing onboarding, and a role that changed can be fixed. We found no study with a stated sample and collection period that measures how often struggling sales hires recover, so this page publishes no recovery rate.

What does waiting on a struggling sales hire cost?

Waiting is the only branch with no end date, which is what makes it the most expensive of the three. The cost accrues in management time, the pipeline that does not build, and the accounts a struggling seller is holding, and it climbs every month the decision stays open. Revenue Bench publishes no single total for waiting, because the value of the territory is usually the largest term and it is specific to the company. For a floor, DePaul University's Center for Sales Leadership, in its 2011-2012 Sales Effectiveness Survey of 435 B2B sales organizations, puts the average cost of sales turnover around $49,508.

What should change in the search after a sales hire does not work out?

Rewrite the role definition from the employer audit, turn the identified failure into an explicit screen, and assess every candidate at the same stage. Build the missing management conditions before the next start date.
