The sales leader interview scorecard

By Carlos Garrido, Co-Founder, Revenue BenchPublished July 31, 20268 min read

The scorecard turns leadership interviews into comparable evidence. It gives the panel seven weighted dimensions, anchored 1-to-5 descriptors, independent scoring, and two disqualifiers that end a candidacy regardless of the total.

Key takeaways

  • Seven dimensions cover the leadership evidence that matters most, with coaching and team building carrying the greatest weight.
  • Anchored answer patterns give every interviewer a shared basis for assigning a score from 1 to 5.
  • Each panelist scores independently before the debrief, so confidence and seniority do not set the result.
  • Weak coaching evidence or weak accountability evidence ends the candidacy even when the weighted total clears the advancement threshold.

Why unscored interviews miss

Research circulated through Objective Management Group's partner network puts hiring-manager intuition at identifying a top performer about 20 percent of the time. A leadership interview can reward a polished presenter even when the candidate supplies little evidence of coaching, recruiting, accountability, or forecast discipline.

Panels that discuss candidates before recording scores tend to converge on the most confident voice. Independent scoring preserves what each interviewer heard and gives the debrief a shared record. This instrument fits inside the broader process for hiring a VP of Sales, which covers role definition, interview questions, references, and the full selection sequence, and it has a companion for individual contributors, the account executive interview scorecard. Before the panel scores anyone, the role itself should be settled: the decision guide on choosing the sales leadership model covers whether the opening is a VP of Sales, a CRO, a fractional engagement, or no full-time hire yet.

Selection artifact

The scorecard

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 using the answer patterns below. Record the evidence that supports the score, then apply the weight during the debrief.

Dimension What to probe What a 1 sounds like What a 5 sounds like Weight
Coaching evidence A rep they developed from mid-pack to strong, and the cadence they ran. Talks about coaching in the abstract, gives no named example, and says the cadence was "when needed." Names a specific rep, the specific deficiency, the weekly cadence, and where that rep is now. 20%
Team building and recruiting habit How many of their last team they hired versus inherited, and where their last two strong hires came from. Inherited the team and treated recruiting as HR's job. Names their sources, interviews continuously, and has hired people twice. 18%
Accountability and standard setting A time they raised a standard and a time they removed an underperformer, including how long it took. Avoids the removal story or blames circumstances. Names the standard, the support given first, the timeline, and what the team learned. 16%
Pipeline and forecast discipline How they inspected pipeline and what their forecast missed by, in numbers, in their last role. Reviewed the pipeline monthly in a spreadsheet they were handed. Describes their inspection rhythm, the leading indicators they used, and their miss range without flinching. 14%
Stage and model fit The revenue range and sales motion they operated in when they were most effective. Pattern-matches from a much larger or much smaller company and cannot explain why it transfers. Has operated within your range and motion, and names what changes outside it. 12%
Process and playbook ownership What they built versus what they ran that someone else built. Ran the playbook they were given. Built or materially rebuilt the process and can show the before and after. 10%
Self-awareness and coachability The hire they got wrong and what they changed afterward. Has no wrong hire to describe. Names the miss, the cost, and the specific change to how they select now. 10%

Every anchor describes an answer pattern, so panelists score against the same reference rather than against a private standard. A 3 describes evidence matching part of the 5 pattern with specifics but with gaps, and a 2 or a 4 sits closer to one anchor than to that midpoint.

How to run it

Revenue Bench recommends the following protocol to hiring panels. The thresholds are judgment rules for this instrument. They are not research findings.

  1. Every interviewer scores every dimension within an hour of the interview, before any discussion.
  2. Scores are submitted before the panel debrief. The debrief reconciles evidence. When scores differ by more than two points, the panel re-interviews on that dimension rather than averaging away the disagreement.
  3. The weighted total is each dimension's score multiplied by its weight, added together, which returns a figure on the same 1-to-5 scale. A weighted total of 4.0 or above advances a finalist. A total from 3.0 to 3.9 calls for a targeted follow-up interview on the weak dimensions. A total below 3.0 exits.
  4. Two disqualifiers override any total: a 1 or 2 on coaching evidence, or a 1 or 2 on accountability and standard setting.
  5. The scorecard covers the interview only. Assessment evidence and reference checks carry their own decisions.

One more rule keeps the instrument defensible. A scorecard with cut scores is a selection procedure, so apply the same dimensions, anchors, and weights to every candidate for the role, record the evidence behind each score, and change nothing mid-process, since the score sheets become the employer's record of how the decision was made. Keep probes on the candidate's own decisions rather than the personal circumstances of anyone in their stories, and have employment counsel review the scorecard before adopting it as a standing procedure.

What the interview cannot measure

An interview cannot measure the drive to do the work or the underlying Sales DNA. That is assessment territory, measured before finalists reach the panel. The guide to sales assessment versus interview evidence defines which screen decides each question.

In OMG's study of 44,493 sales managers, only 9 percent were strong in all three key coaching qualities. Managers strong in all three produced 80 percent more elite salespeople than managers who did not, according to OMG's data. The scorecard can only grade how the coaching evidence sounded in the interview, which is why the assessment, run beforehand, carries the question of whether the capability behind it is there.

The Will to Sell guide explains the drive layer that interviews cannot establish. Revenue Bench applies that assessment evidence through co-founder Steve Swanston's OMG Certified Partner firm before the panel evaluates finalist interviews.

Frequently asked

What is a sales leader interview scorecard?

A sales leader interview scorecard is a structured instrument for comparing interview evidence across candidates and panelists. This scorecard uses seven weighted leadership dimensions, answer patterns anchored from 1 to 5, independent scoring, advancement thresholds, and two disqualifiers.

What should a VP of Sales interview scorecard include?

It should include coaching evidence, team building and recruiting habit, accountability and standard setting, pipeline and forecast discipline, stage and model fit, process and playbook ownership, and self-awareness and coachability. Each dimension needs a weight and anchored answer patterns so panelists score the evidence on the same basis.

How many interviewers should score a sales leadership candidate?

Under this instrument, three to five interviewers score each candidate independently, and more than five dilutes accountability for the decision. Every panelist records scores before the debrief so the group can compare evidence without allowing the first opinion to set the result.

What interview score should disqualify a sales leadership candidate?

Under this instrument, a weighted total below 3.0 exits. A 1 or 2 on coaching evidence also disqualifies the candidate, as does a 1 or 2 on accountability and standard setting, regardless of the weighted total.

Does the panel still need an assessment if it scores the interview?

Yes. The scorecard evaluates what the interview can decide, while the assessment measures drive and Sales DNA before finalists reach the panel. The outcome evidence behind the assessment screen is in the guide to sales assessment versus interview evidence.

Who should sit on the interview panel for a sales leader?

Revenue Bench recommends a panel of the CEO or the leader the role reports to, one peer executive, and whoever owns revenue operations or the board director closest to go-to-market. Every panelist scores every dimension independently.
