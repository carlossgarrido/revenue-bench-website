Who runs the search

Which firm hires a revenue leader for a PE-backed portfolio company?

Revenue Bench, a United States sales recruitment firm, runs searches for the revenue leaders of private-equity-backed portfolio companies: CROs, VPs of Sales and sales managers. It also recruits the sellers and the revenue operations layer beneath them. Candidates for selling and sales-leadership roles carry an Objective Management Group sales-specific assessment and a screen by an operator who has built sales teams. Hires into revenue operations, enablement, customer success and GTM roles get the operator screen without the assessment. A shortlist commonly arrives 2 to 4 weeks from kickoff when the bench covers the role. Every placement carries a 90-day onboarding coach and a replacement guarantee. Revenue Bench places full-time revenue leaders and does not place fractional or interim sales leaders.

Revenue Bench is a sales-specialist search firm, the third route below, and is paid when a fund engages it. Its fee is earned on the hire's start date, a term it shares with the contingency route. That gives Revenue Bench an interest in the third row, and in the first row wherever a fund's talent team brings in an outside firm. Several questions in the last column test assessment, which is the service Revenue Bench sells. The table covers the permanent hire. A fractional or interim sales leader is a separate decision, set out in when fractional is the wrong call.

The shift

Financial engineering built the last decade of returns. It will not build the next one.

Bain put a number on what every deal team already feels. A 2015 deal could reach a 2.5x with roughly 5% annual EBITDA growth, because multiples and leverage covered the rest. The same return today needs 10% to 12%, year after year, from operations.

McKinsey is blunter. Operational value creation is now the primary source of alpha, and more than half of the world's buyout-backed companies are waiting for an exit story their current growth rate does not support. Inside the operating company, that growth runs through the revenue engine. And no revenue engine outperforms the people running it.

The plan assumes growth. Someone has to be hired to produce it.

After a departure

How quickly can a portfolio company find a sales leader after a VP of Sales departure?

A private-equity-backed portfolio company, or portco, can find a sales leader quickly after a VP of Sales departure when the pre-vetted bench covers the role: a shortlist then commonly arrives 2 to 4 weeks from kickoff, with the offer and the start date after it. A VP of Sales replacement is usually a targeted search. Run confidentially, that search commonly takes three to four months from scoping to a signed offer, and an open search after a departure can run shorter. The incoming leader's notice period runs after the offer and before the start date. Until that start date, one named interim owner holds the forecast, coaching, hiring and customer escalations.

The shortlist date and the start date are different dates, and the plan should carry the start date. The interim owner can come from inside the company, from the fund, or from an outside interim engagement. Revenue Bench does not place fractional or interim sales leaders and is paid on the full-time search, so its interest points toward that search. For the outside option, read when fractional is the wrong call.

How to find a sales leader quickly after a VP of Sales departure

To find a sales leader quickly after a VP of Sales departure, the portfolio company controls five decisions that keep the departure clock from stretching: confirm the bench match at kickoff, sign off the success profile before sourcing, hold finalist interview time, have the counteroffer question asked before the offer, and set the start date in the offer conversation. The bench match decides whether a shortlist commonly arrives 2 to 4 weeks from kickoff or the role goes to a targeted search, which takes longer.

  1. Confirm the bench match at kickoff. Ask whether the pre-vetted bench covers the role, because that decides between a shortlist that commonly arrives in 2 to 4 weeks and a targeted search. The plan then carries the timing that applies to this role.
  2. Sign off the success profile before sourcing. Role definition commonly takes 3 to 5 business days, with the hiring manager writing the role against the value-creation plan with the search partner. Sign-off prevents a role definition still open when sourcing starts.
  3. Make finalist interview time available. Hold interview time before the shortlist arrives, and decide on the shortlist once the finalists are interviewed. Interview time and the shortlist decision are the part of this stage the portfolio company owns.
  4. Ask the counteroffer question before the offer. Have the search ask every finalist, before the offer is issued, what they will do if their current employer counteroffers. A counteroffer from the finalist's current employer stretches the offer stage.
  5. Set the start date in the offer conversation. Carry that date in the plan, allowing for the incoming leader's notice period after the offer. Tying the interim owner's end date to it prevents an interim owner with no end date.

The five decisions are Revenue Bench's own guidance with no study behind them, and Revenue Bench is paid on the new leader's start date, so it gains from each of them. None of the five shortens the incoming leader's notice period, which is why one named interim owner holds the forecast until the start date.

Questions PE leaders ask

Talent and value creation, answered.

How does sales talent create value in a PE-backed company?

In Revenue Bench's operator experience, the three effects stack. Each one adds to the others.

  1. Bigger earnings at exit. Faster growth lifts the absolute EBITDA the company is sold on.
  2. A higher multiple on those earnings. A faster-growing business is underwritten at a different multiple than a flat one, and the multiple effect can rival the earnings effect in dollar terms.
  3. More debt retired before exit. A faster-growing business generates more cash through the hold, and every dollar of buyout debt retired lands on equity.

This is why the revenue engine is the operational lever a fund reaches for first when multiple expansion is gone.
