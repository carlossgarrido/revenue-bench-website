The evidence base

Sources: every statistic on this site, with its sample, period and limits

Revenue Bench cites external statistics across its guides and service pages. This index names the party behind each figure, records the available sample and period, defines the point it supports, and states the limits applied wherever the figure appears.

By Carlos Garrido. Published 2 September 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Every external figure published on this site appears below with the party that produced it and the period its data covers.
  • Objective Management Group produced its own figures and sells the assessment they describe.
  • The DePaul turnover costs use 2011 data from a survey of 435 B2B sales organizations.
  • Figures without a published sample or collection period are listed with those gaps.
  • Twenty-eight sources sit behind the figures on this site.

SHRM, 2025 Benchmarking Survey, Recruiting report SHRM, a membership body for human resources professionals, which sells the full benchmarking reports to its members. 2,371 SHRM members responded to an electronically fielded survey of a random sample of active members, conducted between January 9 and March 3, 2025. The data are not weighted, members holding a vice president or higher position were deliberately oversampled, and respondents were not required to answer every metric, so the sample behind an individual figure varies. Only 20% of organizations track quality of hire; and average cost per hire of $5,475 for a nonexecutive hire against $35,879 for an executive hire. Published on: Sales recruiter vs hiring in-house. What any single company spends or measures, and anything sales-specific. The respondents are SHRM members reporting on their own organizations rather than a random sample of employers, and the figures are self-reported.
DePaul University's Center for Sales Leadership, 2011-2012 Sales Effectiveness Survey DePaul University's Center for Sales Leadership, a university research center. 435 B2B sales organizations. 2011 data. The report is no longer served at its original address and survives on the Internet Archive. The average cost of sales turnover near $49,508, rising to roughly $115,000 fully loaded; and 33% of all sales turnover happening in the first year of tenure. Published on: Why sales hires fail, The true cost of a bad sales hire, and other guides. The 33% figure counts a share of total turnover and never a share of hires. The study has not been repeated in that form.
Dow Scott and Thomas D. McMullen, "Current Thinking on Counteroffers", WorldatWork Journal, volume 26 number 3, Fall 2017 Two academic authors publishing in the WorldatWork Journal, with the survey administered by Korn Ferry Hay Group. 120 rewards and human resources professionals, surveyed in April 2017. 3% have a formal counteroffer policy, 84% decide case by case, and 73% rate their own counteroffer practices not effective or marginally effective. The full set of counteroffer-practice figures from the same survey, including the frequency, retention-impression and relationship findings, is published on Your VP of Sales resigned: should you counteroffer? What happens to people who accept a counteroffer. The answers are self-reported impressions of the respondents' own organizations rather than measured retention outcomes, and the survey's roles-counteroffered scale places sales at 19%.
Nick Toman, Bryan Kurey and Dave Lingebach, Harvard Business Review, October 2024 Three authors publishing in Harvard Business Review. Not stated on this site. Only the figures readable outside the paywall are published, and the sample size and company-revenue range sit behind it. Average chief revenue officer tenure of about 25 months, and 62% of companies see revenue growth decline or stay flat in the fiscal year following a chief revenue officer change. Published on: Keep, coach, or replace an inherited sales leader, and Your VP of Sales resigned: should you counteroffer? Causation cannot be assigned to the chief revenue officer change. The figures describe what follows a change. A chief revenue officer role is also not identical to every sales-leadership role.
US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach The US Bureau of Labor Statistics. News release 26-1152-ATL, published 7 July 2026, covering May 2025. Sales and related occupations at 10.8% of local employment against 8.6% nationally, a location quotient of 1.25; sales engineers at a location quotient of 0.54 with 510 employed; technical and scientific product representatives at 0.92 with 4,730 employed. The full nine-occupation table, with employment, location quotient and mean annual wage for each, is published on Sales recruiters in Miami. How many sellers are available to hire. The release counts employment in an occupation. Its annual wages are the hourly mean multiplied by 2,080 hours and exclude the self-employed.
Bain, McKinsey and Goldman Sachs, 2026 private markets research Three firms publishing annual research on private equity. All three sell services to the industry they report on. Annual reports published in 2026. Bain's Global Private Equity Report 2026, McKinsey's Global Private Markets Report 2026, and Goldman Sachs' Private Markets Outlook 2026. The annual EBITDA growth a 2.5x now demands, given as 10% to 12% against roughly 5% in 2015; 16,000 buyout-backed businesses held past four years, 52% of global inventory; and Goldman Sachs' description of the earnings growth returns now require as low double digits, which the page publishes as the phrase because no numeral is given. Published on: Hire a revenue leader for a PE-backed portfolio company, The private equity operating partner's sales-hiring playbook, and Sales-leader compensation in a PE-backed company. Anything about sales hiring. These describe conditions across private equity, and the connection to a sales-leadership hire is Revenue Bench's argument rather than any report's finding.
CSO Insights, ramp time CSO Insights, a sales-research firm. None stated on this site. No sample size or collection period was found in the source material, so none is given. 40% of companies say a new rep needs 10 or more months to reach full productivity. Published on: Why sales hires fail, and The true cost of a bad sales hire. How long a specific hire will take to ramp. No confidence interval can be attached to it, because no sample or period is published.
SBI Growth, sales turnover rate SBI Growth, a revenue-growth advisory firm. None stated on this site. No sample size, collection period or study title was found for this figure, so none is given. Sales first-year turnover runs about 30%, roughly 2.5 times the 13% rate for employees overall. Published on: Why sales hires fail, and The true cost of a bad sales hire. What turnover a particular company should expect. It also describes a different population from the DePaul turnover figures, and the two are never combined in one calculation.
Paycor, employer guidance updated October 2025 Paycor, a payroll and human resources software company. None published. Employer guidance rather than a study, updated in October 2025. Replacement at roughly $4,000 and three months for an employee in general. Published on: Your VP of Sales resigned: should you counteroffer? What replacing a sales leader costs. The page that carries it states in the same passage that the figure describes an employee in general and is far below what a sales-leadership vacancy costs.
Top Echelon, 2019 survey of its recruiting-network members Top Echelon, a recruiting network, surveying its own members. Members of Top Echelon's own recruiting network, average tenure about 15 years. Published in 2019. 90 days is the most common guarantee window at 44.9%, with 30 days at 20.3% and 60 days at 20.0%; replacement with no money back is the majority remedy at 61.4% against a full refund at 8.4%; and 95.9% of the recruiters surveyed offered a guarantee of some kind. Published on: How to evaluate a sales recruitment agency for the role you are filling, and Placement guarantee terms. What employers received. Recruiters described the terms they offered, and the respondents were drawn from one network rather than the market.
Revenue Bench, September 2026 sweep of search firm websites Revenue Bench, reading the public websites of other search firms. 28 sales and executive search firm websites a United States buyer reaches through ordinary searches for a sales recruiting firm. 26 returned enough copy to read. Read on 7 September 2026. 11 of the 26 readable sites stated that a guarantee exists, 6 stated a period, 7 described the remedy in a sentence, 4 stated what voids coverage, and 1 stated the date the window runs from. Published on: Placement guarantee terms. What a buyer can establish about guarantee terms by reading firms' websites. The sample is neither random nor complete, and the list of firms is not published because this site does not name other firms in its category, so the count cannot be audited from outside. It measures website disclosure and says nothing about the quality of any firm's guarantee or about what an agreement contains.
Staffing Industry Analysts, North America Staffing Company Survey 2021 Staffing Industry Analysts, surveying staffing firms. Staffing firms in North America. Survey year 2021. No respondent count is published. The most common direct-hire fee is 20% of salary, reported by 42% of staffing firms, and 20% is also the median across all segments, with segment midranges of 15% to 20% for commercial staffing, 18% to 22% for professional staffing, and 20% to 25% for direct-hire firms. Published on: Sales recruiter fees, and How to evaluate a sales recruitment agency for the role you are filling. What a sales search costs. The survey covers direct-hire staffing across segments, and 2021 is the collection year.
Korn Ferry and Heidrick and Struggles annual reports Two listed executive-search firms, describing their own pricing in their filings. Korn Ferry's annual report for the fiscal year ended April 2025, and Heidrick and Struggles' annual report. The retained standard of approximately one third of the estimated first-year compensation for the position being filled, plus a percentage for engagement expenses. Published on: Sales recruiter fees, and How to evaluate a sales recruitment agency for the role you are filling. What a retained sales search costs outside executive search. The description is scoped to executive search and comes from firms that sell it.
Clockwork Recruiting, Executive Search Performance Benchmark Report, October 2018 Clockwork Recruiting, the applicant tracking system provider whose customer projects form the dataset. 23,970 anonymized search projects run January 2015 to August 2018 and filtered to retained projects only. Across Clockwork's own customer base of search firms using its applicant tracking system, for projects run January 2015 to August 2018, the average placement rate was 71%; outcomes were 71% placed, 22% cancelled, and 6% internal hire; average days to placement was 123; and placement rates were 60% at the least senior band against 78% for C-level searches. Published on: How to evaluate a sales recruitment agency for the role you are filling. An industry-wide placement rate, a causal effect of seniority, or an exact sales placement rate. The January 2015 to August 2018 data comes from Clockwork's own customer base rather than a random industry sample, and the report plots Sales near the global average without publishing an exact Sales figure.
Vendux, 2024 State of Fractional Sales Leadership Report Vendux, a marketplace that places fractional sales leaders, reporting on the market it sells into. More than 1,000 assignments, self-reported by more than 900 fractional sales leaders on the publisher's roster and through open distribution, collected in December 2024 and merged with the publisher's own records. An average assignment length a little under 8 months, an average of about 12 hours a week, and 4.3 assignments per leader per year. Published on: When fractional sales leadership is the wrong call. Any outcome, satisfaction, early-termination or conversion measure. Every respondent is a fractional leader and no client was surveyed, so the report describes the shape of an engagement and measures nothing about how engagements end. A 2025 edition exists, and its methodology sits behind a lead form, so this site cites the 2024 edition.
Mylance, fractional executive engagement structure, August 2026 Mylance, which sells positioning tools to independent fractional operators, reporting engagements those operators submitted. 191 engagements from 152 independent operators, self-reported, collected over more than one year, with the publisher's own duplicate-removal notes stated in the article. A median of 40 hours a month, and 13% of engagements running above 80 hours a month. Published on: When fractional sales leadership is the wrong call. Anything specific to sales leadership. The engagements span executive functions, so this site draws only the shape of the hours from it, and never a classification outcome or a sales-specific figure.
OMG's 2024 validation survey, selection outcomes Objective Management Group, from its own validation work on candidates its assessment screened. This site does not give a sample size for the 2024 validation survey, because the source material cited does not publish one. The figures are the 2024 set. 72% of candidates OMG recommended reached the top half of their sales force within 12 months; first-year turnover of 9% for recommended hires against 33% for candidates OMG advised against; and 100% of candidates OMG advised against who were hired anyway landed in the bottom half. Published on: Why sales hires fail, The private equity operating partner's sales-hiring playbook, and other guides. Selection outcomes cannot be read as evidence that an assessment reduces turnover or improves a hire. The comparison sets candidates the assessment recommended against candidates it advised against, so it describes selection rather than a measured effect on any hire.
OMG's predictive validity figure Objective Management Group, stated in its assessment documentation rather than in a published study. OMG's own assessment documentation. No sample is published for the validity figure. Predictive validity in the 95 percent range. Published on: How it works, How to assess sales candidates before hiring, and Sales assessment vs interview. Instrument accuracy does not mean that 95% of candidates reached the top half of a sales force. This is the accuracy metric for the instrument and a separate measure from the 72% top-half figure. The two are never given together as one claim.
OMG's assessment dataset, the talent distribution and the instrument Objective Management Group, from the assessment records it has accumulated since 1990. Nearly 2.4 million assessed salespeople as of 2023. No collection period is published for the distribution or the instrument. The dataset itself, which OMG describes as the largest sales-specific dataset in existence; the distribution of 6% Elite, 11% Strong, 33% Serviceable and 50% Weak; OMG's shorthand that about 74% are ineffective to some degree; and the instrument itself, 21 sales-specific competencies across more than 400 data points and roughly 250 questions per candidate. Published on: The 6% problem, Sales team due diligence for private equity buyers, How it works, and other guides. Population figures cannot establish that the distribution holds inside one company. It describes the assessed population. The four-tier split and the 74% shorthand are two OMG framings of the same population and are never summed or set against each other. The superlative belongs to OMG and is attributed on every page that carries it.
OMG's evaluation of 44,493 sales managers Objective Management Group, from an evaluation of sales managers rather than salespeople. 44,493 sales managers. No collection period is published for it. 9% are strong in all three coaching qualities, and managers strong on all three have 80% more elite salespeople on their teams. Published on: Should you promote your best rep into sales management?, and The first 100 days. Coaching qualities cannot be identified as the cause of the elite salespeople. The comparison is between managers who hold all three and managers who do not.
OMG's coaching study Objective Management Group, from an evaluation of salespeople alongside the managers who coached them. 11,078 salespeople and their managers. No collection period is published for it. About 20% of salespeople are coached weekly and about 10% several times a week, and coaching several times a week gains 17 points of sales percentile against no coaching. Published on: The 90-day sales onboarding plan, The 30-60-90 day scorecard, and other guides. The study cannot establish that adding coaching sessions produces 17 points. It compares groups at different cadences.
OMG's before-and-after training study Objective Management Group, measuring salespeople before and after training delivered by one of its certified partners. 5,331 salespeople measured before and after training by a certified OMG partner. No collection period is published for it. Will to Sell moved 0% to 6% after training, while tactical skills moved further, with closing up 55%, reaching decision makers up 43% and consultative selling up 28%. Published on: Will to Sell, How to assess sales candidates before hiring, and Keep, coach, or replace an inherited sales leader. Anything about sales managers. The measured population was salespeople, and the pages that use it for a leadership argument state that limit.
OMG's Sales DNA and competency prevalence data Objective Management Group, from its evaluation population across the windows named in each figure. OMG's evaluation population. Individual figures carry the windows their pages name: 27% strong on Sales DNA overall across more than 1,500 evaluations from 2023 to 2025; 12% strong and 88% weak on supportive beliefs across 2022 to 2025; 11% to 15% strong on consultative selling and 16% proficient in sales technology in 2025. The desire and rejection figures carry no window. 27% strong on Sales DNA overall; 12% strong and 88% weak on supportive beliefs; 86% strong on desire; 88% to 89% strong on handling rejection; 11% to 15% strong on consultative selling; 16% proficient in sales technology. Published on: The 6% problem, Why sales hires fail, and How to assess sales candidates before hiring. Competency shares cannot predict one candidate's performance. These describe the assessed population and set the base rate a screen works against. The 88% figure carries two different meanings on this site, a strength on handling rejection and a weakness on supportive beliefs, so it is never quoted without naming the competency.
OMG's single-seller concentration figure Objective Management Group, from a count of sales teams rather than individual salespeople. 318 sales teams. No collection period is published for it. 30% of sales teams rely on a single salesperson for more than half of revenue. Published on: Building a sales talent bench, Sales team due diligence for private equity buyers, and other guides. This measurement cannot show whether concentration is rising or falling. The figure is one measurement of one set of teams with no trend behind it.
OMG's motivation data Objective Management Group, from its evaluation population of salespeople. OMG's evaluation population of salespeople. No sample size or collection period is published for it. Most salespeople are intrinsically motivated and about a quarter are extrinsically motivated. Published on: Sales compensation benchmarks for 2026, and Sales-leader compensation in a PE-backed company. How a specific individual is motivated, or that the split holds for sales leaders. The measured population was salespeople.
OMG's role-fit data Objective Management Group, from its evaluation population, with no study named for these two figures. OMG's evaluation population. No sample size or period is published for these two figures, and none is stated on this site. 57% of salespeople are in roles they are not suited for, and 64% of sales teams are significantly misaligned. Published on: Why sales hires fail. A given team cannot be described as misaligned from these figures alone. The figures describe the assessed population.
Hiring-manager intuition, Big Swift Kick The firm Big Swift Kick. The research was circulated in Objective Management Group's partner network and is not an OMG published study, which the pages that use it already state. None published. Hiring managers spot a top performer about 20% of the time. Published on: Sales assessment vs interview, and How to assess sales candidates before hiring. An 80% interview-failure rate cannot be inferred from the figure. No sample or method is published for it, and every page that carries it names it as partner-network research.
The mis-hire working figure Revenue Bench. It is the firm's own working estimate and is labeled as such wherever it appears. Revenue Bench labels it as an estimate rather than a measurement. No sample or period applies. A revenue-role mis-hire costs $200,000 to $250,000, counting base compensation, draw, the pipeline that stalled while the role underperformed, the replacement search, and management time. Published on: The true cost of a bad sales hire, and Sales recruiter vs hiring in-house. Research does not support the number. It is Revenue Bench's estimate, and the third-party figure on the same question is DePaul's, which is separate and lower because it counts different items.
The total sales cost range Revenue Bench. It is a planning range the firm uses when sizing a sales budget, and it is labeled as such on the page that publishes it. This is Revenue Bench's planning range rather than a benchmark. It carries no sample or period. 15% to 25% of new revenue as total sales cost. Published on: Sales compensation benchmarks for 2026. Industry benchmark status cannot be assigned to the range. The page publishes no salary table and labels the range as the firm's own working figure.

Who produced each figure

  • Independent research, government statistics and published journalism. This class includes academic work, government data, annual private-markets research, sales research, and employer guidance. Each source is named with its period and available sample.
  • Surveys and filings from parties inside the staffing and search industry. Top Echelon surveyed members of its own recruiting network, Staffing Industry Analysts surveyed staffing firms, Korn Ferry with Heidrick and Struggles described executive-search pricing in their own filings, and Clockwork Recruiting reported outcomes from search projects run by its own applicant-tracking customers.
  • Reports published by parties who sell what the figures describe. The fractional sales leadership figures come from a marketplace that places fractional leaders, and the fractional engagement-hours figures from a company that sells positioning tools to independent operators. Both are self-reported by the operators themselves, and neither surveys a client, so this site uses them for the shape of an engagement and never for an outcome.
  • Objective Management Group's own data. Objective Management Group produced these figures and sells the assessment they describe. The figures are that group's own published data rather than independent peer-reviewed research.
  • Revenue Bench's own figures and frameworks. Revenue Bench's quantified planning figures are labeled as the firm's own estimates or ranges. Its frameworks and instruments carry no external statistic and are labeled on the pages that publish them. One figure in this class counts what other firms publish on their own websites, and because this site does not name other firms in its category, the sample behind that count is not published and a reader cannot audit it. It is stated with that limit wherever it appears.

Figures considered and not published

Revenue Bench considered the figures below for this site and traced each one to its source. None of them resolved to a source that publishes a sample and a period, so none is published here.

Revenue Bench does not publish a counteroffer retention percentage, a bad-hire cost stated as a share of first-year salary, a one-year placement-success rate, an executive-hire failure rate, a contingency search success rate, a search-performance figure presented as covering the whole category, a failure rate for fractional executive engagements, or a rate at which fractional engagements convert to full-time hires. Each was traced between August and September 2026 and the trail ended without a study, a stated sample, or a collection period. The fractional failure rate in widest circulation resolves to a social post, and the conversion rate in circulation is published by a firm that sells the conversion.

One search-performance dataset did resolve to a stated sample and period, and it is published rather than withheld. Clockwork Recruiting's Executive Search Performance Benchmark Report of October 2018 is listed in the index above and used with its limits on how to evaluate a sales recruitment agency for the role you are filling. It describes Clockwork's own customer base of search firms using its applicant tracking system rather than the category, which is why no category-wide rate is drawn from it.

Two of those trails are already published in full on the pages where the question arises. The counteroffer retention figure and where its trail ends are set out on how to replace a VP of Sales confidentially, and the bad-hire cost range is addressed on the true cost of a bad sales hire.

How figures are stated on this site

  • Every quantified claim names its source, gives the sample where the source publishes one, and gives the period the data covers.
  • Where a source is behind a paywall, only the figures readable outside it are published, and the page says so.
  • Objective Management Group's outcome figures compare candidates the assessment recommended against candidates it advised against. No page states that an assessment reduces turnover.
  • Predictive validity in the 95 percent range is the accuracy metric for the instrument. It is never given as the share of candidates who reached the top half of a sales force, which is a separate measure.
  • A figure whose data carries a year is published with that year attached.
Frequently asked

Common questions

Which statistics on this site are independent research and which are not?

The sources fall into four groups: independent research, government statistics and published journalism; surveys and filings from parties inside the staffing and search industry; Objective Management Group's own data; and Revenue Bench's own quantified figures, frameworks and instruments. Each row states the producer's relationship to the figure.

How large is the sample behind the Objective Management Group figures?

The dataset is nearly 2.4 million assessed salespeople as of 2023. Specific studies use narrower sets, including 44,493 sales managers and 11,078 salespeople and their managers in the coaching study. This site does not give a sample size for the 2024 validation survey because the source material cited does not publish one.

What does predictive validity in the 95 percent range measure?

Predictive validity in the 95 percent range is Objective Management Group's accuracy metric for its sales assessment. It is a different measure from the share of recommended candidates who reached the top half of their sales force within 12 months. Revenue Bench keeps those measures separate and applies the accuracy phrase only to OMG's stated metric.

Why does the DePaul turnover cost figure carry a 2011-2012 date?

The date reflects the data behind the source. DePaul University's Center for Sales Leadership ran the 2011-2012 Sales Effectiveness Survey across 435 B2B sales organizations using 2011 data. The study has not been repeated in that form. Its original address no longer serves the report, which survives on the Internet Archive.

Are there statistics Revenue Bench decided not to publish?

Yes. Revenue Bench traced eight candidate figures and left them unpublished after their trails ended without a stated sample or period: a counteroffer retention percentage, a bad-hire cost given as a share of first-year salary, a one-year placement-success rate, an executive-hire failure rate, a contingency search success rate, a search-performance figure presented as covering the whole category, a failure rate for fractional executive engagements, and a rate at which fractional engagements convert to full-time hires. One search-performance dataset did resolve to a stated sample and period and is published with its limits: Clockwork Recruiting's October 2018 report, which covers its own customers rather than the industry. The section above sets out what was checked.

Can I check these sources myself?

Yes. Every source in the index is named in full, and the ones with a public address are linked from the table. The DePaul report survives only on the Internet Archive, so it is named rather than linked. The Harvard Business Review piece is partly behind a paywall, so Revenue Bench publishes only the figures readable outside it.
